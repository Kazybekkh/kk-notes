# pi0: A Vision-Language-Action Flow Model

**Paper:** [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.24164) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2410.24164)  
**Start with:** [[Robot Learning Primer]] | **Related:** [[Cross-Embodiment Transfer - 2607.27549]], [[Diffusion Policy - 2303.04137]], [[LeRobot - 2602.22818]]

## In plain English

pi0 asks how one policy can follow language instructions and control several kinds of robots. It starts with a pretrained vision-language model, which can interpret images and text, and adds an action-generation mechanism based on **flow matching**. Training combines data from single-arm, dual-arm, and mobile-manipulator robots.

## The main idea

A vision-language model has broad knowledge of objects and instructions but does not inherently know how to move a robot. The action part learns to produce continuous robot controls. Flow matching is a generative method: it learns a path from a simple noise distribution toward plausible actions. This is related in spirit to [[Diffusion Policy - 2303.04137]], though the training and sampling methods differ.

## What the abstract covers

The paper evaluates zero-shot tasks after pretraining, language instruction following, and adaptation through fine-tuning. Examples include laundry folding, table cleaning, and box assembly. The abstract does not give a small-hardware recipe; a final-year project should treat pi0 as a reference architecture, not an assumed baseline that can be trained from scratch.

## What to inspect in the PDF

Find the action representation shared across robots, data mixture, model size, inference frequency, fine-tuning requirements, and test conditions. Ask which parts of its generalization come from the model and which from the breadth of training data.
