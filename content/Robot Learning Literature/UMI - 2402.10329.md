# UMI: Universal Manipulation Interface

**Paper:** [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2402.10329) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2402.10329)  
**Start with:** [[Robot Learning Primer]] | **Related:** [[ACT and ALOHA - 2304.13705]], [[RealDexUMI - 2606.06033]], [[UMI-on-Air - 2510.02614]]

## In plain English

Robot demonstrations are usually recorded by moving an actual robot. UMI asks whether a person can instead carry a handheld, gripper-shaped device through an ordinary environment, record useful demonstrations, and later train a policy that runs on a robot. That makes data collection more portable and potentially cheaper.

## How the pieces fit

The handheld gripper records human manipulation in a form designed for robot deployment. The policy represents actions as **relative trajectories**, such as how the gripper should move from its current pose, rather than only absolute coordinates tied to one setup. The authors also match training and deployment latency: a controller that receives observations late must be trained with that delay in mind. The abstract reports dynamic, bimanual, precise, and longer-horizon real-world behaviors across multiple platforms.

## Terms to know

**In the wild** means data collected outside a tightly controlled robot lab. **Hardware-agnostic** means the policy interface is intended to transfer across robot platforms; it does not mean every robot can execute every demonstrated motion. **Zero-shot generalization** here concerns deployment to novel environments or objects without retraining for those tests.

## What to inspect in the PDF

Track exactly what sensors the handheld device records, how camera and gripper poses are calibrated, how demonstrations become robot action targets, and what robot-specific setup remains. A realistic SO-101 question is whether a cheaper collection device still supplies enough pose accuracy for one chosen task.
