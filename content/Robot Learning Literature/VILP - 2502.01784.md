# VILP: Imitation Learning with Latent Video Planning

**Paper:** [arXiv abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2502.01784) | [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2502.01784)  
**Start with:** [[Robot Learning Primer]] | **Related:** [[Diffusion Policy - 2303.04137]], [[Robot Learning from Human Videos Survey - 2604.27621]]

## In plain English

Rather than predicting robot actions immediately, VILP first predicts what future camera views might look like. A policy can use these imagined frames to plan. The video predictions live in a **latent space**, a compressed numerical representation rather than raw pixels, to make modeling more efficient.

## What the abstract reports

The method generates temporally aligned future video from two viewpoints and is compared with other video-generation and imitation approaches. The abstract reports advantages in training cost, inference speed, temporal consistency, and policy performance in its experiments. It also suggests less reliance on extensive high-quality task-specific action data, which should be checked against the exact datasets and baselines.

## Terms to know

**Video diffusion** generates a sequence of frames by iterative refinement. **Temporal consistency** means the same objects and motions remain coherent across frames. **Latent planning** reasons over compressed predictions of the future.

## What to inspect in the PDF

Identify how imagined frames are converted into actual control, whether the tests involve real hardware, and how prediction errors affect long tasks. For a small robot, compare against a direct imitation baseline before adding video prediction.
