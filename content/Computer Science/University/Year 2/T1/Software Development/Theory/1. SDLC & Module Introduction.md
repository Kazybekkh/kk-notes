## Software Development Lifecycle (SDLC)

### Introduction to SDLC
**SDLC Context**: 
- More detailed coverage in **ECM2434 – Group Software Engineering Project**
- **Testing** (key SDLC component) covered in this module

### SDLC Definition
**Software Development Lifecycle**:
- **Represents development** of software as phases or sets of activities
- **Driven by desire** to define predictable process to improve quality  
- **Involves creating processes** that can be effectively managed

### Basic SDLC Phases

#### Traditional Linear Flow
```
Requirements Analysis → Design → Implementation → Testing → Evolution
```

#### Phase Descriptions

##### 1. Requirements Analysis
**Key Questions**:
- What **business/scientific problem** is the system addressing?
- **Is the solution feasible**?
- **What will the system do**?
- **Who are the users**?

##### 2. Design  
**Key Questions**:
- What are the **hardware, software, and network requirements**?
- What parts will be **in-house development**?
- What will be **externally developed** or **bought off-the-shelf**?

##### 3. Implementation
**Actual coding** and system construction

##### 4. Testing
**Verification and validation** of system functionality

##### 5. Evolution
**Key Questions**:
- **Has the system accomplished** its goals?
- **Are there modifications** to be made?
- **If so, return to point 1** (iterative improvement)

## Software Development Methodologies

### Methodology Selection
**Multiple SD methodologies available**, selection depends on:
- **Project type**
- **Available resources**  
- **Team capabilities**
- **Client requirements**

### Major Methodologies

#### 1. Waterfall
#### 2. Prototyping  
#### 3. Rapid Application Development (RAD)
#### 4. Agile

## Waterfall Methodology

### Characteristics
**Sequential Approach**:
- **Breaks SDLC into distinct sequential phases**
- **Each phase needs sign-off** (approval) before moving to next
- **Very well-documented** projects
- **Linear progression** through phases

### Advantages
- **Clear structure** and milestones
- **Comprehensive documentation**
- **Predictable timeline**
- **Easy to manage** and understand

### Disadvantages
- **Errors propagate** through if not spotted early
- **Very expensive to fix** errors discovered late
- **Inflexible to change**
- **No working software** until late in process

### When to Use Waterfall
**Suitable for**:
- **Well-understood requirements**
- **Stable technology** environment
- **Experienced teams**
- **Regulatory compliance** projects

## Prototyping Methodology

### Characteristics
**Iterative Approach**:
- **Not complete methodology** but way of tackling larger stages
- **Approaches exist across design areas** 
- **Involves end users** during process
- **Lower functionality prototypes** constructed for learning and feedback

### Process
- **Analysis, design, and implementation tightly coupled**
- **Prototypes feed back** to development process
- **Can cause initial plans** to be reassessed
- **Often prototypes are discarded** after learning

### Benefits
- **Early user feedback**
- **Risk reduction** through experimentation
- **Better understanding** of requirements
- **Validation of concepts** before full implementation

## Rapid Application Development (RAD)

### Characteristics
**Tool-Focused Approach**:
- **Heavy focus on tool use** to reduce development time and costs
- **IDEs, GUI builders, database management systems**
- **Planning and implementation interleaved**
- **Business need paramount**

### Priority Structure
- **Development deadline prioritized** over requirements
- **When project iterations overrun**: Focus on **reducing requirements** rather than **extending deadline**
- **Speed to market** emphasis

### Trade-offs
- **Faster delivery** vs **comprehensive features**
- **Tool dependence** vs **custom solutions**
- **Rapid results** vs **long-term maintainability**

## Agile Development

### Core Philosophy
**Agile Manifesto Principles**:
- **Individuals and interactions** over processes and tools
- **Working software** over comprehensive documentation  
- **Customer collaboration** over contract negotiation
- **Responding to change** over following a plan

### Characteristics
- **Software development practice** based on **short iterative "sprints"**
- **Adaptive** rather than predictive
- **People-centric** approach
- **Embraces change**

## Scrum Framework

### Sprint Structure
**Sprint Characteristics**:
- **Short fixed-length sprints** (typically 2-4 weeks)
- **Aim to produce deployable software** in each sprint
- **Time-boxed iterations**

### Scrum Meetings

#### 1. Sprint Planning Meeting
**Purpose**: Plan upcoming sprint
**Activities**:
- **Identify which items** will be worked on
- **Identify sprint tasks**
- **Divide sprint into time-boxes**

#### 2. Daily Scrum Meeting  
**Purpose**: Coordinate daily work
**Activities**:
- **Summarize previous day's work**
- **Plan coming day's work**
- **Foster collaboration** among team members

#### 3. Sprint Review Meeting
**Purpose**: Demonstrate and evaluate sprint results
**Activities**:
- **Demonstration of completed software**
- **Project owner determines** which work items are complete
- **Gather feedback** for next sprint

## Pair Programming

### Structure
**Two-Person Collaboration**:
- **Driver**: Actually typing the code
- **Navigator**: Responsible for overall direction of programming
- **Roles periodically reversed** between the two

### Benefits

#### 1. Productivity
- **Better productivity** than two programmers working independently
- **Fewer bugs** introduced
- **Higher code quality**

#### 2. Knowledge Sharing  
- **Promotes dissemination** of project knowledge among team
- **Cross-training** on different parts of system
- **Reduced bus factor** (knowledge concentration risk)

#### 3. Skills Development
- **Good for transferring skills** to junior developers
- **Mentoring opportunities**
- **Best practices sharing**

## Advantages of Agile Development

### 1. User Engagement
- **Continuous user involvement**
- **Regular feedback** and course correction
- **User satisfaction** through involvement

### 2. Rapid Development
- **Quick delivery** of usable product
- **Early value** to users
- **Faster time to market**

### 3. Quality Assurance
- **Early testing catches bugs** and results in high-quality product
- **Continuous integration**
- **Regular validation**

### 4. Risk Management
- **Small incremental changes** mitigate potential risk
- **Early detection** of problems
- **Adaptive response** to changes

## Choosing a Development Model

### Factors to Consider

#### 1. Project Type
- **Complexity** and **size**
- **Innovation level** required
- **Technology maturity**

#### 2. Team Factors
- **Your preferences** and experience
- **Team abilities** and skills
- **Team size** and distribution

#### 3. Organizational Factors
- **Employer preferences** and culture
- **Customer preferences** and involvement
- **Regulatory requirements**

#### 4. Hybrid Approaches
- **May combine traits** from multiple methodologies
- **Tailored approach** to specific project needs
- **Best practices** from different models

## Exercise: Pair Programming Benefits

**Question**: What are the benefits of pair programming?

### All Options Are Correct

**A.** The observer can consider the "strategic" direction of the work, coming up with ideas for improvements and likely future problems to address.
- ✅ **Correct**: Navigator focuses on big picture and future considerations

**B.** The driver can focus all their attention on the implementation and coding.  
- ✅ **Correct**: Driver concentrates on tactical coding details

**C.** It's a good way of transferring skills to junior developers.
- ✅ **Correct**: Effective mentoring and knowledge transfer mechanism

**D.** A person can be responsible for mistakes.
- ❌ **Incorrect interpretation**: Pair programming is about **shared responsibility**, not individual blame

**Note**: Option D is misleading - pair programming promotes **collective ownership** and **shared responsibility** rather than individual accountability for mistakes.

## Key Takeaways

### SDLC Importance
1. **Structured approach** to software development
2. **Quality improvement** through defined processes  
3. **Risk management** through systematic phases
4. **Predictable outcomes** with proper methodology selection

### Methodology Selection
1. **No one-size-fits-all** approach
2. **Project characteristics** drive methodology choice
3. **Hybrid approaches** often most effective
4. **Team capabilities** influence success

### Agile Benefits
1. **Adaptive** to changing requirements
2. **User-centric** development approach
3. **Risk reduction** through incremental delivery
4. **Quality focus** through continuous testing

### Pair Programming
1. **Improved code quality** through collaboration
2. **Knowledge sharing** across team
3. **Skill development** for junior developers
4. **Strategic and tactical** thinking balance
