# 18 Master's, research engineering, and a startup

[[Home]] | Previous: [[17 Finding a Research Question]]

Your goals support one another when you build durable evidence of skill. Strong coursework helps you understand papers; a careful project demonstrates research ability; reliable implementation helps a team; understanding deployment failures reveals possible customer problems.

## Build an application from evidence

Prioritize mathematical and CS fundamentals, sustained work with a supervisor, clear technical writing, and a project you can explain deeply. A recommender who has observed your reasoning and persistence can describe more than a list of technologies. Michigan Robotics explicitly lists academic performance, research experience, recommendation letters, and the statement of purpose among its admissions considerations. [Official admissions guidance](https://www.robotics.umich.edu/academics/graduate/admissions/).

Create a portfolio containing a small reproducible baseline, an original controlled experiment, an honest failure analysis, and a concise report. Show what you implemented and what came from existing tools. A result with a careful negative finding can still demonstrate research maturity.

## Choose a program by fit

These are starting points for investigation, not a ranking or an admissions prediction:

| Program family | Question to investigate | Official starting point |
|---|---|---|
| CMU MS in Robotics | Thesis research, advisor access, and lab fit | [CMU robotics master's programs](https://www.ri.cmu.edu/education/academic-programs/masters/) |
| CMU MRSD | Whether systems and product-oriented project work matches your research goals | [MRSD FAQ](https://mrsd.ri.cmu.edu/faq/) |
| Michigan Robotics MS | Directed study, available supervisors, and pathway into substantial research | [Degree requirements](https://www.robotics.umich.edu/academics/graduate/degree-requirements/) |

CMU's own FAQ distinguishes MSR's thesis emphasis from MRSD's combination of technical projects and management preparation. Verify current prerequisites, cost, funding, deadlines, and research access directly for your application year. A program's prestige alone does not ensure access to the advisor or project you want.

Your personalized shortlist still needs your expected graduation date, academic record, finances, citizenship-related constraints, and preferred research style. Track these privately; none has been assumed here.

## Prepare for a robot-learning team

Study the work of [Physical Intelligence](https://www.pi.website/) through primary papers such as [[pi0 - 2410.24164]]. Build relevant capabilities: dataset debugging, distributed-training literacy, policy evaluation, robot deployment, and clear experiment design. These are suggested preparation areas, not a claim about a current vacancy or a promise of employment.

Write one-page technical memos: problem, method, key evidence, failure modes, and the next experiment. Learn to inspect another person's code and produce a small well-tested improvement. Research scientist, research engineer, and robotics systems roles can have different qualification expectations.

## Develop startup judgment alongside technical skill

Ask who experiences an expensive repeated problem, what the current workaround costs, and whether autonomy changes the economics. Measure reliability, recovery burden, throughput, installation effort, and support cost.

$$
\text{Value per period}\approx
\text{labor or downtime avoided}
-\text{hardware, operation, maintenance, and support cost}.
$$

This is an accounting prompt, not a business forecast. A research benchmark and a paying customer's workflow are different evaluations. Use projects to learn from users before assuming a general robot is the first viable product.
